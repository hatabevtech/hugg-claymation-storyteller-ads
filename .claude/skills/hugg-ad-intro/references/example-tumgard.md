# Worked example: YOGABODY "Fix Your Feet" → TumGard × H. pylori

## Reference intro (up to the product)
"Do you have painful feet? Plantar fasciitis or bunions? Flat feet, Morton's neuroma? Overlapping or hammer toes? Maybe Achilles tendonitis? Podiatrists always want to sell expensive inserts. Doctors want you to wear orthopedic shoes that look like ski boots. The internet wants you to walk barefoot and foam roll your arches throughout the day. Now, all of these interventions would be fine if they worked, but the reality is your feet still hurt. That's why you're watching me now. Here's what you need to know: 30% of adults have a diagnosable foot problem. That's the bad news. You're not alone. The good news is there's a very good chance you can fix your feet. … I call these the three S's … in my upcoming Fix Your Feet Masterclass."

## Literal first draft (what NOT to stop at)
"Antibiotic ka course poora kiya… phir bhi jalan? Khane ke baad jalan? Khatti dakaar? Pet bhaari-bhaari? Raat ko seene tak aag? Ya report negative, par pet abhi bhi wahi? Ghar walon ne kaha: chai band. Doston ne kaha: mirchi band, sirf khichdi. Internet ne kaha: subah jeera paani. Aur jeb mein hamesha ek antacid. Itna sab chhoda… aur pet phir bhi shaant nahi. Isiliye aap yeh dekh rahe ho. Toh suno. [X]% Indians mein H. pylori hota hai. Aap akele nahi ho. Antibiotic ne bacteria hata diya…"

## User's final
"H. pylori gaya, phir bhi jalan? Pet mein jaise patthar. Khatti, badbudaar dakaar. Khatta paani muh mein. Seene mein jalan. Har khane se pehle darr. Ghar walon ne kaha: chai band. Doston ne kaha: mirchi band, sirf khichdi. Internet ne kaha: subah jeera paani. Yeh sab karne ke baad bhi - issue nahi gaya matlab problem plate me nahin, pet me hai. 80% Indians mein ek bacteria hai jo pet ki lining ko sensitive karta hai. Good news: bacteria hatao, pet 30 din mein khud recover hone lagta hai. Usi bacteria se ladne ke liye, humne banaya hai TumGard."

## Why each change was made
| Beat | Change | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | "course poora kiya" → "H. pylori gaya" | Name the label the viewer already owns (like "painful feet"); outcome, not process; sets up the contradiction. |
| List | Clinical words → similes and senses ("jaise patthar", "badbudaar") | Gut sufferers don't know diagnosis names; they know feelings. Agitate, don't describe. |
| List | Scene details cut; 5 short lines | Every viewer matches every line; fast cuts. |
| List | Ends on "darr" | Escalates from body to mind; emotional cost hits hardest. |
| List | "Report negative" dropped | The hook already says it. |
| Tried | Antacid removed | Rule of three; it's a medicine, not advice from people; it isn't a plate fix, so it would break "plate me nahin, pet me". |
| Turn | "That's why you're watching me" and "Toh suno" cut | Speaker-centred filler; an AI host has no earned authority. |
| Turn | "Doesn't work" → "issue nahi gaya, problem plate me nahin, pet me hai" | Gives the reason, not just the failure; takes blame off the viewer; quotable; no knock on remedies. |
| Stat | Prevalence → prevalence plus what the bacteria does | The stat becomes evidence for the turn. |
| Stat | "Aap akele nahi ho" dropped | 80% says it. |
| Stat | "H. pylori" → "ek bacteria" | Widens the audience; plain-language villain. |
| Good news | Vague → "30 din mein khud recover" | Concrete, simple, the body does the work. |
| Product | Mechanism section and framework cut; "Usi bacteria se…humne banaya hai TumGard" | Product arrives as the direct answer at about 30 s instead of 50 s. |

## Open flags at the time
- "80%" and "30 din mein khud recover" needed sources.
- "Usi bacteria se ladne ke liye" moved TumGard from "supports the lining" to "fights the bacteria", which needed a claims decision.
- The hook says the bacteria is "gaya" while the product line fights it, so the chain didn't fully close.
