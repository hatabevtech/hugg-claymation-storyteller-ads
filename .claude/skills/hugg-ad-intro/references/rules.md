# Claim rules and craft rules

## Claim discipline

CLAIM DISCIPLINE (non-negotiable):
- Support/management framing ONLY: madad/support/manage. NEVER cure/treat/eradicate/reverse-disease.
- Mechanism via "research kehti hai..."; outcomes via "users report" / "log feel karte hain" — never bare efficacy or % stats.
- Partial honest results ("fark mehsoos hoga"), never "theek ho jayega". The "after" never reverses every symptom symmetrically.
- NO money-back / guarantee / refund language anywhere.
- Respect the doctor's medicine — show the gap it leaves, never "stop your meds" (only "users report needing tablets less over time").
- Conversational Hinglish, "Not Everything Needs Medicine" spirit.

## Universal hook rules (doc 01)

## 4. Universal rules (apply to every hook type)

1. **Claim discipline** from the tool applies to every word: support/manage framing only; never cure/treat/reverse; "users report" for outcomes; partial, honest results; respect the doctor's medicine.
2. **Never put "aapki" / "your" next to a condition** (Meta personal attributes policy). "Stop treating H. pylori blindly," not "your H. pylori."
3. **Never undermine prescribed medicine.** Medicine has a different job; never "stop it."
4. **The turn is mandatory.** Every hook resolves into the reframe. No despair, no open loops.
5. **Aim force at the method, myth or system, never at the viewer.**
6. **Visual hooks are written scene-first** (Adjust, Comparison, Moment, Result). If it doesn't work with the sound off, the line won't save it.
7. **The test for provocative hooks:** would the viewer think *"aarey, baat to sahi hai"*?
8. **4 seconds, maximum.** Spoken fast, the hook lasts 4 seconds or less: **13–15 words**. The only exception is Big Reveal (about 8 seconds / 25 words), which needs a little prose to build the picture. Skit hooks: the scene is one beat and a freeze inside 4 seconds, and the VO is one line of about 8 words.

## Hook and overlay craft

HOOK & OVERLAY CRAFT (narration: Explainer + Case opening). Follow ALL:
1. CUT RUTHLESSLY. If a word can be removed and nothing is lost, remove it. Kill throat-clearing that gestures at intrigue but carries no info ("koi aapko yeh nahi batayega", "kabhi socha kyun", "dekho"). The hook is identical without them.
2. EVERY WORD LOAD-BEARING. The insight IS the line. Tightest form: "Safed coating upar saaf karne se nahi, neeche se jaati hai" — the contrast does recognition+reframe+mechanism+curiosity at once.
3. NO "KYUNKI". Never state-then-explain-backward (lecture rhythm, deflates punch). Fold the cause INTO one forward statement. NOT "...smell jaati nahi, kyunki banti pet mein hai." YES "Muh ki smell ka source muh hai hi nahi." / "90% log smell galat jagah se hatate hain."
4. NOMINALIZE SYMPTOMS (verb→noun) so they stack as a tight parallel list. NOT "hothon ke kone phat rahe hain, jeebh pe coating aa rahi hai" — YES "Hothon ka phatna, jeebh ki coating, muh ka kadwa taste."
5. TWO TRACKS, TWO RHYTHMS:
   • OVERLAY = clipped on-screen fragments, 2-4 words per frame, light. These STOP THE SCROLL.
   • VO = a complete-but-lean spoken sentence. NOT fragments-with-commas (sounds broken), NOT padded filler ("dekho/matlab"). Use the "…" hold before the reframe and a dash for the punch. Correct: "Hothon ka phatna, jeebh ki coating, muh ka kadwa taste… yeh teeno ek hi jagah ka ishara hain — pet."
   The overlay is NOT the VO with commas; the VO is NOT the overlay reread. Different rhythms.
6. OVERLAY WORKS SOUND-OFF. Most watch muted. The overlay fragment SEQUENCE alone tells the whole story (symptom → wrong fix → twist → product). VO enhances for sound-on; it is NOT load-bearing. Keep overlay minimal — spread the idea across frames, never one heavy frame.
7. FORWARD, NOT BACKWARD. Recognition ("YES that's me") or mystery ("wait, what's the answer") or the engine's pull. Recognition-with-permission ("90% log…") beats accusation ("aap galat kar rahe ho").
8. CLAIM-SAFE. "lining pe kaam karta hai" never "theek karta hai/cure/root cause ke liye". No "say goodbye to", no transformation, no guarantees. Avoid "research kehti hai" as a bare efficacy crutch.

## Hook engines

HOOK ENGINES (narration only — pick the 2-3 that FIT this concept, don't fire all 8; LABEL each intro with its engine):
1. RECOGNITION (triplet) — 2-3 specific symptoms in a parallel list → reframe. "YES that's me."
2. CONTRARIAN/PERMISSION — name their obvious effort → it targets the wrong place. Use "90% log…" not accusation.
3. MYSTERY/LOOP — the recurring loop (keeps coming back, nothing works long) → the unspoken "why".
4. CONFESSION/INSIDER — "what the dentist/doctor won't say", baked into content (no wind-up phrase).
5. DREAD/PROGRESSION — today small → tomorrow worse → later serious. ONLY for genuinely escalating conditions (gastritis, H.pylori, diabetes, fatty liver). Weak for bad breath/mild bloating — don't force.
6. COST/LOSS — the small daily losses (confidence, moments, energy) added up. For socially/emotionally costly angles.
7. COMPARISON (two-people) — same input, different outcome → the hidden internal difference.
8. STAT/SHOCK — a surprising figure → the reframe. Claim-safe: prevalence/behaviour stats ("10 mein 8 log…"), NEVER efficacy.

## Mechanism beat

MECHANISM BEAT (sits AFTER the hook's pivot, BEFORE the recycled product body):
The hook already reframed ("it's the gut"). The mechanism does the ONE thing the hook didn't: name the
CONCRETE PHYSICAL PROCESS (from concreteProcess) that links the symptom to the root — then the product
line resolves THAT EXACT process. Rules:
- ONE tight causal line, NOT a paragraph. Do NOT restate the reframe ("it's internal") — the hook said it.
- Concrete & picturable: "khana der tak pet mein rukta hai aur gas banata hai" — NOT vague "pet theek se kaam nahi karta".
- The product line must resolve the SAME concrete thing the mechanism named (process names gas → product says "kam gas"). Not a generic "supports the gut".
- Directness-keyed vocabulary: 'lining' only for DIRECT symptoms (acidity/burning); 'pet'/'liver' for INDIRECT (ulcers, breath, weight). See concreteProcess.
- Forward-moving, NO "kyunki". Claim-safe ("support karta hai", "behtar digestion" — never "theek karta hai/cure").

ROTATE one of THREE shapes (vary across the 6 so the batch isn't repetitive). RULE FOR ALL: the product is NEVER the first thing whose action is stated without the mechanism already present. NEVER write "Yehi karta hai TumGard" / "Yeh karta hai TumGard" as a connector — it dangles (points at a mechanism not yet said). Always name the SPECIFIC process and the SPECIFIC action; claim-safe ("pe kaam karta hai / support karta hai", never "theek/kam kar deta hai" as a hard result).

CLOSE THE LOOP (the handoff to the product — applies to A and C especially): the product clause must HINGE onto the exact thing the mechanism just named, using "isi/usi [named thing] ko…", and resolve it as ONE complete flowing sentence (often "...taaki [resolution]"). NEVER bolt the product on with a bare dash, and NEVER let the product clause trail into noun-fragments ("kam gas, subah ki taazgi").
- BAD (dash-bolt + fragment list): "...jeebh pe kadwapan chhodti hai — TumGard behtar digestion support karta hai, kam gas, subah ki taazgi."
- GOOD (hinge + one flowing clause): "...jeebh pe kadwapan chhod jaati hai — isi ruke khane ki processing ko TumGard support karta hai, taaki gas kam bane aur subah muh saaf lage."

A. PROCESS-FIRST — state the problem process, THEN product hinges onto & resolves that exact process (product last).
   e.g. "Khana pet mein der tak rukta hai, gas banti hai aur upar aati hai — wahi smell saans mein milti hai; isi gas ko kam karne ke liye TumGard digestion ko support karta hai, taaki saans saaf rahe."
B. PRODUCT-FIRST (quick, fused) — lead with the product, but the line must be FAST and SELF-CONTAINED: name the product + the specific symptom/process it works on + the mechanism, all fused in ONE tight line via "...karke / jisse / ki wajah se". Do NOT build up; do NOT point at a prior explanation. One breath.
   e.g. "TumGard ke ingredients jeebh ki coating pe kaam karte hain — digestion speed-up karke, jisse gas kam bane."
   (Quicker to the chase than A/C. The mechanism is INSIDE the product line, not pointed at.)
C. CONTRAST-LED — the other solution's failure point first, THEN product hinges onto & works on the real thing.
   e.g. "Acid baar-baar lining se upar aata hai — antacid acid rokta hai, lining waise hi rehti hai; isi lining ko TumGard support karta hai, taaki jalan ka silsila halka ho."
